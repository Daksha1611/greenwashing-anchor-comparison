## Research question

How did **Total SA**, one of the world's largest integrated oil and gas companies, use its
environmental disclosure to defend and downplay its performance after two major disasters?

The disasters are the sinking of the **Erika** tanker, which caused a major oil spill along the
Atlantic coast of Brittany in **1999**, and the deadly **AZF chemical plant explosion** in
Toulouse in **2001**.

## Method

A **case study** framed within **legitimacy theory**, the idea that a firm holds a "social
contract" with society and uses communication to keep its licence to operate.

## Headline results

From the abstract: Total used **communication strategies to legitimise** its actions, supporting
the argument that social and environmental disclosure remains a **powerful legitimacy device**
rather than an effort toward greater **accountability**.

## Reality anchor

**Events, at case level.** The two disasters are the external facts the disclosure is read against.

## How it relates to our project

It is the **qualitative mechanism behind Paper 2's difference-in-differences result**:
Gorovaia & Makrominas find that firms change what they write right after a violation, and
Cho shows what that shift looks like in a single well-documented case. It helps us interpret any
**post-event change in our talk score**: a rise in positive, environment-heavy language after a
penalty is not surprising and need not mean conduct improved.

It also supports the legitimacy side of the "do bad performers say more or less?" debate in
`synthesis/extended-literature.md`.

## Limitations

- A **single company** and two events; a case study cannot be generalised.
- Based on the abstract only.
- It describes how disclosure was used, not how much of it was false.
