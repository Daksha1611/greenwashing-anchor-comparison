## Research question

† What kind of firm greenwashes in its ESG disclosures, and what deters it?

## Method

† Greenwashers are firms that **look very transparent**, disclosing large quantities of ESG data,
but **perform poorly**. The paper builds **peer-relative greenwashing scores** for **1,925
large-cap firms** across countries.

We can confirm the *structure* of the measure from two papers we have read:

- Luu et al. (2025, note 12) describe the measure they adopt from Yu et al. as the **standardised
  ESG disclosure score minus the standardised ESG performance score**.
- Wang et al. (Paper 4) describe Yu et al. as having developed "a relative greenwashing index
  based on the disconnection between disclosure and performance". Paper 4 also lists a related
  approach from another group using Bloomberg scores, which is the same type of measure.

† The brief says the standardisation is done **within industry and year**. We have not been able
to check that.

## Headline results

† Scrutiny deters greenwashing: **independent directors, institutional investors, a less corrupt
country system and cross-listing** are all associated with less of it. No coefficients are given
in our brief, and we state none.

## Reality anchor

**Ratings gap**: a commercial disclosure score minus a commercial performance score. Both halves
are third-party scores, so neither is a direct measurement of conduct.

## How it relates to our project

- **The template that later papers copy.** Luu et al. (note 12) and Liu et al. (note 15) use this
  measure. It is probably the most common way empirical finance and accounting papers
  operationalise greenwashing, which is why our repository must treat the ratings gap as an anchor
  type of its own (`synthesis/comparison.md`).
- **Add it as a fourth index.** We propose a **GSI-ratings** index alongside our three, because it
  is what applied finance actually uses; the question is whether it agrees with the harder anchors
  (hypothesis H5).
- **A good standardisation model.** Peer-relative scoring (standardising within industry-year)
  would help stop sector differences in emissions or newsworthiness from driving agreement or
  disagreement between our indices. That is Improvement 8.

## Limitations

† Our reading from the brief and from how others describe it: a ratings gap is only as good as
the two ratings it differences. If vendors' disclosure scores reward quantity of data and
performance scores reward size and data availability (see notes 23 and 24), the gap partly
measures rating methodology. Not checked against this paper.
