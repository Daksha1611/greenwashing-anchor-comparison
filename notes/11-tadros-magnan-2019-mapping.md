## Research question

How does a firm's environmental performance shape what it discloses? In particular, do the
economic incentives to disclose and the legitimacy pressures to disclose work differently for
**high-performing** and **low-performing** firms?

## Method

**Panel data analysis** (the same firms followed over many years) of firms in **environmentally
sensitive industries**. The paper looks at three disclosure channels at once: the **annual
report, the 10-K and the sustainability report**, and examines how performance interacts with
economic and legitimacy factors.

From the abstract: **78 firms over 14 years**. † 1997–2010, US firms.

## Headline results

From the abstract:

- There appears to be a **reporting bias based on environmental performance**: **high performers
  disclose more** environmental information across the three channels combined.
- **Economic and legitimacy factors influence the disclosure decisions of low and high performers
  differently.**
- **There is little evidence that low performers use their disclosures to legitimise** their
  environmental operations.

## Reality anchor

**Environmental performance.** The abstract does not say which performance measure is used; we
have not seen it, and we do not guess. (To check in the paper.)

## How it relates to our project

1. **10-Ks alongside sustainability reports.** The study treats SEC 10-K filings as one of the
   disclosure channels. This supports our plan to use **SEC EDGAR text as a supplementary "talk"
   source** next to sustainability reports (see `synthesis/datasets-and-access.md`).
2. **It reinforces note 10's reversal of the legitimacy prediction.** Again, good performers say
   *more*, and there is little sign that bad performers talk their way out. Paper 2 finds the
   opposite for volume and tone, so the sign depends on how "disclosure" is measured; see the
   contradictions in `synthesis/extended-literature.md`.

## Limitations

- Based on the abstract only; the performance measure, coefficients and the full set of controls
  are unknown to us.
- 78 firms in environmentally sensitive industries is a small, selected sample.
- It is a statement about *how much* firms disclose, not about whether the disclosure is true.
