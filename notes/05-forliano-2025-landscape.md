## Research question

Not an empirical study — a **systematic literature review**. It asks: what does the greenwashing
research field actually look like, which theories does it lean on, and where are the gaps?

> A *systematic literature review* follows a pre-registered, reproducible search-and-screen
> procedure rather than citing whatever the authors happen to know. *Bibliometric analysis* then
> studies the citation and keyword structure of that corpus statistically.

## Method

- **97 peer-reviewed articles** from **Scopus and Web of Science**, selected by stated inclusion
  and exclusion criteria, in business and management.
- Bibliometric analysis + network analysis to derive thematic clusters.
- A **full-text** reading of all 97 to map which theoretical frameworks each one uses — the part
  that distinguishes this from a routine bibliometric exercise.

## Headline results

**Four thematic clusters:**
1. Symbolic management and CSR communication
2. Environmental regulations and institutional complexity
3. Performance and sustainable practices
4. Marketing, perception, and trust

**A multi-level theoretical framework** — macro (societal/institutional), meso
(organisational/inter-organisational), micro (individual perception and decision-making).

**Macro-level theories dominate.** Legitimacy theory, institutional theory and stakeholder theory
are the primary frameworks. Four theories — **institutional, legitimacy, signaling and stakeholder**
— operate across all three levels.

The authors note the field skews toward *pragmatic* legitimacy (meeting immediate stakeholder
demands) and call for work on the moral and cognitive dimensions of legitimacy.

## Stated limitations

- Restricting to Scopus and Web of Science may exclude grey literature and adjacent fields.
- Confined to business and management literature.
- Cluster identification involves interpretation; different analysts might cluster differently.
  The authors explicitly call for better validation techniques for cluster identification.

## Why this paper matters for our project

It is the **field-level map** — it tells us where our question sits and confirms the gap is real
rather than an artefact of our reading three papers.

Two things to be careful about, though:

1. **It is a theory map, not a measurement map.** Its organising question is which *theories* the
   literature uses, not which *measurement anchors*. It does not systematically compare
   media-anchored, violation-anchored and emissions-anchored operationalisations of greenwashing.
   So it establishes the field's shape and its heterogeneity, but it **does not itself demonstrate
   our specific gap**. We should cite it for context and for the absence of such a comparison —
   not as evidence that the comparison has been shown to matter. Claiming more would overstate it.
2. **Coverage window.** With articles accepted in Feb 2025, it cannot include Davidescu et al.
   (2026) or Wang et al. (2025). Our two most methodologically relevant papers post-date the map.

**Useful supporting point:** the review documents real construct heterogeneity across the field —
consistent with our concern that different greenwashing measures may not be measuring the same
thing. That is the strongest legitimate use of this paper in our argument.
