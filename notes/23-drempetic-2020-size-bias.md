## Research question

† Are commercial ESG scores biased in favour of **large firms**?

## Method

† Using **Asset4** (Refinitiv) ESG ratings, the paper examines how **firm size**, the **resources
available for producing ESG data**, and **data availability** relate to a firm's ESG score.

## Headline results

† Size, resources for ESG reporting and data availability all **raise a firm's ESG score**, which
favours larger firms. No figures are given in our brief.

**A follow-up (not one of our 24 papers).** Dobrick, J., Klein, C., & Zwergel, B. (2023). Size
bias in Refinitiv ESG data. *Finance Research Letters, 55*, 104014.
https://doi.org/10.1016/j.frl.2023.104014. † Our brief says it reports that the bias persists in
Refinitiv data. We verified the citation in Crossref but have not read the paper.

## Reality anchor

**Not applicable.** This is a study of **rating bias**, not a greenwashing measure.

## How it relates to our project

It is **strong prior evidence for our salience hypothesis (H3), applied to commercial ratings**.
If size and the resources to produce data inflate a rating, it is plausible that the same forces
inflate media-based and disclosure-based greenwashing measures, because large firms are more
visible, more written about and better at reporting. Our H3 regression should therefore **control
for size** in the same way.

It also fits the third contradiction in `synthesis/extended-literature.md` (are large firms
cleaner?): ratings rise with size for reasons that may have nothing to do with conduct.

## Limitations

† Pending the paper: a size bias in a rating shows the rating favours large firms, but it does
not by itself say whether larger firms are truly better or worse. Separating the two needs an
independent measure of conduct, which is the reality-anchor idea.
