## Research question

Different ESG data providers rate the same firms differently. Is that **disagreement** itself
related to stock returns?

## Method

ESG ratings from **seven different providers** for firms in the **S&P 500**, **2010–2017**. The
authors study the relationship between rating disagreement and stock returns.

## Headline results

From the abstract:

- **Stock returns are positively related to ESG rating disagreement**, suggesting a **risk premium
  for firms with higher disagreement**.
- The relationship is **primarily driven by disagreement about the environmental dimension**.
- The authors discuss implications for firms' cost of equity and for investors who use ESG
  strategies.

† Also from the brief (not in the abstract): the **average pairwise correlation between
providers is about 0.45**, and disagreement is **higher for the largest firms**.

## Reality anchor

**Not applicable.** The paper compares **raters with each other**, not with an outside measure of
conduct.

## How it relates to our project

This is the **methodological model** for our study. It does for ESG raters what we propose for
greenwashing measures: take several measures that claim to describe the same thing on the same
firms and measure how much they agree.

- **A benchmark for "moderate" agreement.** † If the average pairwise correlation between raters
  is about 0.45, that gives a reference point for judging our own Spearman correlations: a value
  in that range would put greenwashing measures at about the level of agreement ESG raters
  already have, which is a poor standard for something that is supposed to be a single concept.
- **A sharper version of H3.** † If disagreement is concentrated among the **largest firms**, our
  prediction becomes that **anchor disagreement** (how far a firm's standardised scores differ
  across our indices) is higher for larger and more media-covered firms.
- It supports the **MTMM-style** reading of our design: one trait (greenwashing), several methods
  (anchors) (Improvement 2).

## Limitations

- Based on the abstract only; the figures marked † have not been confirmed.
- The study is about *ratings*, and disagreement among raters is a different thing from
  disagreement among greenwashing indices; the analogy is a design guide, not a result.
- Disagreement alone does not say which rater is right.
