## Research question

How is corporate greenwashing related to **corporate governance** features that normally reduce
agency problems (board independence, gender diversity, board size)? And does greenwashing affect
firm value?

## Method

The authors propose a **novel set of measures of greenwashing** and relate them to board
characteristics. † The brief says the sample is US firms and that the measure contrasts ex-ante
intentions with ex-post outcomes. Neither point is in the abstract.

## Headline results

From the abstract:

- **Firms with more independent directors tend to greenwash more.**
- **The presence of female directors** seems to have a **positive impact on the degree of
  greenwashing** (the abstract does not say more, and we do not interpret the direction further).
- The effect of **board size** on greenwashing is **ambiguous**.
- **Greenwashing reduces firm value.**

## Reality anchor

**Unverified.** † If the brief is right, a controversy-based gap (ESG score versus controversy
score). The brief's description of the measure as contrasting ex-ante intentions with ex-post
outcomes is also absent from the abstract, so it is unconfirmed too.

## How it relates to our project

- **If confirmed, a controversy-anchored measure for US firms**, the same market as ours. It
  would give us a ready-made fifth index (Improvement 1) whose real-world side is media-derived.
- **A second example of validation against a market outcome** (firm value), alongside note 15's
  crash-risk result; relevant to our predictive-validity idea (H6).
- **A caution carried over from note 16:** controversy scores come from news, so a
  controversy-anchored index may inherit the visibility problem.
- Note 20 builds on this measure (as supplied).

## Limitations

- Based on the abstract only. We do not know the sample, the construction, the effect sizes or the
  robustness checks.
- A surprising result (more independent boards, more greenwashing) may reflect how the measure is
  built as much as behaviour; we cannot judge that without the method section.
