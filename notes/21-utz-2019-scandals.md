## Research question

† When a company is hit by a scandal, do commercial ESG ratings react? In particular, can a
company's aggregated ESG assessment warn us that a scandal is coming?

## Method

† An **event study** (a method that looks at how a variable behaves around a dated event) on
**press releases of corporate scandals**, using **Asset4** ESG data from an international
sample of firms.

## Headline results

† Around the scandal period, **retrospective controversy indicators drop and then rebound**;
**forward-looking indicators rise slightly**; and **aggregated ESG assessments are of little use
for predicting scandals**. No figures are given in our brief and we state none.

## Reality anchor

**Media events (scandals)**, used to test the ratings. The scandal dates give an outside,
time-stamped fact against which a measure's behaviour can be judged.

## How it relates to our project

It supplies the **method for an event-response test**. A measure that claims to capture
misconduct should *move* when a real scandal or enforcement action becomes public. We propose to
apply this to our indices using dated external events such as formal EPA enforcement actions
(Improvement 3 in `synthesis/combining-and-improvements.md`). A media index that moves with
coverage spikes but not with enforcement events would be tracking attention, not conduct.

It also adds to the evidence (with notes 17 and 23) that **aggregate ESG ratings are a poor
guide to conduct**.

## Limitations

† Our reading, pending the paper: scandal press releases are an event set chosen by what became
public, so they over-represent visible firms; and an event study shows the *timing* of
changes, not whether any measure was right about the underlying conduct.
